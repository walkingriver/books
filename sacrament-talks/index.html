How to Give a Sacrament Talk When You Really Don't Want To

Sound Familiar?

  • The bishop called you on Tuesday and sacrament meeting is Sunday
  • You get nervous just thinking about standing at the pulpit
  • You wonder whether anyone will want to hear what you have to say
  • You don't know where to start when planning a talk
  • You want to do well—but you really don't want to do this

Here's the truth: you don't need to be a gifted orator. You need a simple process and the courage to use it.

Not a Performance. A Chance to Share.

Sacrament meeting talks are part of worship for members of The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints. They are also one of the most common sources of Sunday-morning anxiety in the building.

I still feel those butterflies—even after years of speaking and training others. The difference is knowing what to do with them.

This book is short on purpose. You do not have time for a semester of public speaking theory. You need something you can read tonight and use this week: how to calm down, pick one message, prepare content that invites the Spirit, and walk back to your pew knowing you did your part.

I have made every mistake I warn about in these pages. That is the point. You should not have to learn them the hard way.

What You'll Learn

Handle the Assignment

What to do the moment the bishop asks—including the emotions that hit before you open your scriptures.

Pick One Message

How to choose a clear theme and build around it instead of cramming in everything you ever thought about the topic.

Prepare Content

Practical steps for scriptures, stories, and structure—without turning your talk into a research paper.

Script or Outline?

The pros and cons of each approach, and how to decide which one fits you and this talk.

Learn from Bad Talks

Real examples of talks that missed the mark—and how to rewrite them into something that works.

Deliver with Confidence

What to do on the day: pacing, eye contact, and trusting the Spirit when your hands won't stop shaking.

Imagine Walking to the Pulpit Knowing...

  • You have one clear message—not a pile of half-formed ideas
  • Your preparation invited the Spirit instead of feeding your anxiety
  • You chose the right format: scripted, outlined, or somewhere in between
  • You avoided the common mistakes that make congregations tune out
  • You can get through the talk sincerely, even when you really didn't want to give it

This is not a topic encyclopedia. It is a field guide for the talk you have to give next—not someday when you feel ready.

About the Author

Michael D. Callaghan is a Latter-day Saint, software developer, and trainer with more than three decades of experience teaching complex topics to people who would rather be somewhere else.

He has given—and coached—plenty of sacrament meeting talks. He wrote this book because the advice he needed when he was nervous at the pulpit was not "just pray about it."

His other books include Don't Say That at Work (communication skills for your career) and P-AI-R Programming (AI-assisted software development).
